The Lion Who Carried the Dawn

A story of sunlight, song and gratitude

✦

Eritrean highlands, between Asmara and Sherigini

Folktale Sunrise over the highlands

Long ago, in a small village in the Eritrean highlands, there lived a young girl named Saba. She lived with her grandmother in a small house surrounded by fields and tall acacia trees. The villagers were farmers, and they depended on sunlight to grow their crops.

However, something strange was happening. Every morning, the village remained dark for much longer than usual. The sun seemed to be disappearing, and the days were becoming colder. The farmers worried that their crops would stop growing.

One evening, Saba sat beside her grandmother and asked, “Why does the sun take so long to rise?” Her grandmother looked towards the hills and told her an old story about a golden lion who carried the dawn. Many years ago, the lion had become unhappy because people stopped appreciating the sunlight. He disappeared into the forest, and ever since then, the mornings had become darker.

Saba was fascinated by the story. She decided that she would find the lion and convince him to bring back the sunlight. The following morning, she packed some food and walked towards the forest. As she passed the acacia trees, she noticed enormous paw prints on the ground. She followed them until she reached a clearing.

Suddenly, she heard a deep growl. “Who is walking through my forest?” a voice asked. Saba turned around and saw an enormous lion with golden fur and bright amber eyes. She was frightened but gathered her courage and asked, “Are you the lion who carries the dawn?”

The lion slowly nodded. “Yes, I am. Long ago, I brought sunlight to the village every morning. But the people stopped appreciating the beautiful days I gave them. They complained about the heat and never noticed how important the sunlight was. So I decided to keep the dawn for myself.”

Saba thought about the farmers, children and animals waiting for the morning. “But the people need sunlight. Without it, the crops cannot grow, and the animals cannot find food. Everyone deserves another chance,” she explained.

The lion looked away and replied, “Words are easy, little girl. How do I know that they truly care?” Saba remembered a traditional Tigrinya song she had heard growing up. She stood in front of the lion and began singing. The familiar melody filled the forest, reminding the lion of the days when the village welcomed every sunrise.

As the song filled the forest, the lion's ears lifted. His golden fur began to glow, and the dark forest slowly became brighter. “I remember that song,” the lion whispered. “It reminds me of the days when the village welcomed the morning with happiness.”

The lion stood up and walked towards a large rock overlooking the hills. Saba followed him carefully. He climbed onto the rock, lifted his head and gave a mighty roar. His roar travelled across the valleys and echoed through the mountains.

Suddenly, a bright golden light appeared on the horizon. The sun slowly rose, spreading warmth across the village. The birds began singing, and the flowers opened towards the sunlight. Saba smiled with happiness. The lion looked at her and said, “You have reminded me that the morning is not something to keep for myself. It is a gift meant for everyone.”

When Saba returned to the village, she told the people what had happened. At first, they found it difficult to believe her story. However, when they saw the bright sunlight, they celebrated and thanked her. From that day onwards, the villagers made sure to appreciate every sunrise. They taught their children to respect nature and never take its gifts for granted.

Some people say that if you listen carefully in the early morning, you can still hear the golden lion roaring from the hills just before the sun appears. And that is why the people of the village believe that the lion continues to carry the dawn.

MoralWe should appreciate the gifts of nature because sometimes we only realise their importance when they are gone.
